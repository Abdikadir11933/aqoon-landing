# Study and work-entry routes

These routes are not interchangeable. Starting study without first checking its effect on unemployment security can affect the person's case. AQOON should identify the person's present route and send them to their own employment authority early, with a clear goal and the provider/course details if already known.

## Authority-organised labour-market training

Labour-market training is organised by the employment authority. It can build vocational or entrepreneurship capability and can include integration training. AQOON may help find and compare official course information; the authority and training provider determine the route and participant selection.


For a person receiving Kela's general social security benefit, Kela's current services page says the benefit is paid during agreed employment-promoting services and can be paid five days a week. The page lists labour-market training, self-motivated study, immigrant self-motivated study, job-search coaching, career coaching and work trials as such services. This describes the published framework, not a personal payment decision.

## Self-motivated study while unemployed

This means study delivered by an educational institution, rather than the employment authority. The official national page distinguishes short-term, part-time and full-time study. For full-time study with unemployment benefit, the employment authority assesses the education need, whether study is the best way to support employment and whether the required criteria are met; it should normally be agreed before study begins. The study route does not remove the person's work-search and service-participation duties.

## Immigrant customer's self-motivated study

For a person using the immigrant/integration customer path, full-time self-motivated study may be supported only where the person is registered as a jobseeker, the authority assesses it as the best route to employment and it is agreed in the integration plan. Examples can include Finnish/Swedish study, basic education, preparatory education and qualification-completion study. These examples are not a placement promise.

## Work trial

A work trial is not employment. It is an authority-agreed way to explore an occupation or support a return to working life. The person must be registered as a jobseeker, it must be agreed in their employment plan and the authority must assess it as the appropriate support. Its duration and working hours are agreed with the authority and organiser; the national page states a maximum of six months.


## Job-search and career coaching

Job-search coaching supports independent job search, including search channels and assessing work options. Career coaching supports career/field choices, applying for training and work-life readiness. Both require jobseeker registration, an authority assessment and an employment-plan agreement. They are not generic AQOON training sessions or automatic course placements.

## Expenses during an agreed service

Kela's current page lists a EUR 9 per-day expense compensation, up to five days per week, for certain employment-promoting services that are agreed with employment services. EUR 18 per day may apply where the published distance/accommodation conditions are met. The listed services include labour-market training, independent study under the Integration Act, job-search/career coaching and work trials. Do not promise the compensation for generic self-motivated study or an individual case: Kela decides payment and its page specifies the included services and conditions.

## Wage subsidy and apprenticeship

Wage subsidy is paid to an employer, not to the person. It may be relevant to a jobseeker's service need, including an apprenticeship route, but the employment authority and employer decide whether it is available. The national page says it is most often 50% of wage costs and commonly granted for five or ten months, with the exact period and amount decided separately for each support period. Availability, local appropriations and any policy changes are high-volatility facts; never tell a person or employer that subsidy is approved before an official decision.
