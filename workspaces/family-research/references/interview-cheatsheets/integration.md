# Integration cheat sheet

Last verified: 2026-08-27. Primary source: Job Market Finland / local integration and employment authorities.

## 1. Osaamisen ja kotoutumisen palvelutarpeen arviointi

**What it is:** An assessment of the person's skills, work/functioning situation, ability to start work/study, language level and what integration services are actually needed.

**Why it matters:** It is often the foundation for deciding whether a kotoutumissuunnitelma and kotoutumiskoulutus are appropriate.

**Paid?** The assessment itself is not salary. It is an authority service.

**Ask:** Has the person had an initial interview/assessment? Was Finnish/Swedish starting level assessed? Did they receive a plan or next appointment?

## 2. Kotoutumissuunnitelma — integration plan

**One-sentence explanation:** “It is a plan made with the authority for the early integration period, agreeing which language, employment, education or other services will help you move forward.”

**What it can include:**
- Finnish/Swedish studies
- literacy teaching
- kotoutumiskoulutus
- job-search or career coaching
- työkokeilu
- other employment/integration services

**Paid?** The plan itself is not a payment. Depending on the person's benefit eligibility and the agreed activity, they may receive unemployment benefit/other support while participating.

**Criteria:** Made according to need after/with the integration service-needs assessment. The responsible authority decides whether and what kind of integration plan is appropriate.

**If client says NO / never had one:** ask:
- How long in Finland?
- Is she a registered jobseeker?
- Has she had any initial integration assessment?
- Is she currently in Finnish/integration training?

Explain: “The plan can be important because it is where the authority agrees the language/training/employment steps. If you do not have one, we can first check whether you should have an assessment or plan.”

## 3. Kotoutumiskoulutus — integration training

**What it is:** Integration-oriented training, usually focused on Finnish/Swedish plus society and working-life skills, with the goal of progressing toward work or further studies.

**Who arranges it:** It can be organised as labour-market training through employment/integration services; exact local route and provider varies.

**Paid tuition?** Labour-market training itself is normally free to the participant. If the person is unemployed, the same unemployment benefit can generally continue during labour-market training, and expense allowance may be possible.

**How to get in:** Usually through the local employment/integration authority after service-needs/language assessment and when it is judged appropriate. Do not tell the person to simply enrol in any course and assume benefits will continue.

**If no:** ask whether the problem is schedule, childcare, course location, literacy, level being too easy/hard, or they want vocational/work-focused Finnish instead.

## 4. Important integration interview distinctions

- **Not all immigrants automatically have the same integration plan/services.** Need, timing and legal/service situation matter.
- **Being in Finland for years does not by itself tell you whether a plan is active or possible.** Check current authority status.
- **A Finnish course is not automatically kotoutumiskoulutus.** Ask who arranged it and whether it is recorded/agreed in the person's plan.
- **Do not assume benefit payment.** Ask what benefit they currently receive and verify the effect of the specific agreed activity.
